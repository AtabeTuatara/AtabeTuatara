# 🔐 Escrow & the need for liquidity

### <mark style="color:blue;">**The need for Escrow**</mark>

When shoppers purchase items, they need to know that the funds are escrowed; in other words, the marketplace did not send the funds to the seller immediately. This fulfills the all important "arbiter of trust" role that shoppers rely on marketplaces for. And to create, and maintain, this trust each and every order and related payment is recorded and shown in Dema’s escrow smart contract on-chain for both seller and shopper to view.

### <mark style="color:blue;">**Without funds to purchase items, how do orders get processed?**</mark>&#x20;

But if the funds are in stuck in the smart contract until the order is processed and the items is received, where do the funds come to acquire the items the shoppers purchased? Someone has to pay the Amazon sellers to acquire those items the Shoppers are expecting.&#x20;

This is where liquidity from the strategic business partners comes into play. They contribute funds to fulfill orders.

Not only should the Dema operators not withdraw the escrowed funds to give the business partners, they cannot.  Dema's smart contract code does not permit this unless specific delivery and processing conditions are met; conditions that are detected automatically in code and since the smart contract cannot be changed, no one can violate the escrow. In other words, the operators at Dema cannot simply move funds. They are bound by the contract's code; code that is also visible to all sellers and shoppers.&#x20;

In summary, these strategic partners overcome the initial supply-demand deadlock acting as "surrogate sellers". They source from the same Amazon sellers the shoppers expected to get their items from.

### <mark style="color:blue;">**What's motivating them?**</mark>

The business partners have two incentives for taking on this measured risk.&#x20;

* They understand & value Dema's long-term strategic plan & the we're aspires to achieve.
* They receive a modest, nearly negligible amount of Dema equity in exchange.
